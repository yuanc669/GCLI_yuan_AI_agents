MicroRNA-100-5p in exosomes from human placental mesenchymal
stem cells: a key modulator of anti-psoriatic effects through mTOR/S6
K1 pathway

Yu‑Chen Huang1,2,3 · Chao‑Yuan Chang4 · Chun‑Jen Huang1,5,6,7

Received: 13 November 2024 / Revised: 23 May 2025 / Accepted: 1 June 2025

Abstract
Exosomes from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) demonstrate therapeutic potential against psoriasis, primarily due to their
anti-inflammatory properties. However, the specific components within the exosomal cargo, particularly microRNAs, that
mediate this activity remain unclear. This study investigated the role of a specific microRNA in mediating the therapeutic
effects of exosomes from human placenta MSCs (MSC-exosomes) in an imiquimod (IMQ)-induced psoriasis murine model,
with a focus on the underlying mechanisms, particularly the downstream pathways of the specific microRNA. Our small RNA
sequencing analysis identified microRNA (miR)-100-5p as a potential target. Adult male Balb/c mice were then randomly
assigned to the IMQ, IMQ plus MSC-exosomes (IMQ + Exo), IMQ plus MSC-exosomes treated with a specific miR-100-5p
inhibitor (IMQ + Exoi), or IMQ plus agomiR-100-5p group (IMQ + AgomiR) (n = 6 per group). Control groups were also
included. Mice were sacrificed, and psoriasis severity was assessed the day after 6 consecutive days of daily IMQ exposure,
followed by 7 consecutive days of daily treatment. The IMQ + Exo group exhibited significantly lower epidermal thickness,
levels of inflammatory cytokines and psoriasis-related cytokines (interleukin [IL]-12, IL-17, and IL-23), and expression of
mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR)/S6 kinase 1 (S6K1) (the predicted downstream target of miR-100-5p), compared
to the IMQ group (all p < 0.05). Notably, inhibition of miR-100-5p nullified these therapeutic effects of MSC-exosomes.
This study provides evidence that miR-100-5p plays a crucial role in mediating the therapeutic effects of MSC-exosomes in
an IMQ-induced murine psoriasis model, potentially by modulating the mTOR/S6K1 pathway.

Key messages
• Exosomes from human placental mesenchymal stem sells demonstrate therapeutic potential against psoriasis.
• MicroRNA-100-5p plays a crucial role in mediating the therapeutic effects of mesenchymal stem cells exosomes in an
imiquimod-induced murine psoriasis  model, potentially by modulating the mammalian target of rapamycin/S6 kinase 1
pathway.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a chronic disease affecting the skin and joints,
driven by genetic factors and inflammatory dysregulation
[1]. It involves abnormal keratinocyte activity and immune
cell infiltration, with T helper (Th) 1 cytokines like tumor
necrosis factor (TNF)-α and interferon (IFN)-γ abundant
in psoriatic plaques [1]. Key cytokines, including interleu-
kin (IL)−12, IL-23, and IL-17 A, are found in the serum,
skin lesions, and blood of psoriasis patients, contributing
to its pathogenesis.1

Exosomes, small extracellular vesicles (30–150 nm),
facilitate intercellular communication by transferring sub-
stances like proteins, RNAs and microRNAs between cells
[2]. They reflect the condition of their source cells and
are implicated in the therapeutic effects of mesenchymal
stem cells (MSCs), particularly in immunomodulation [3].
Studies have explored MSC-derived exosomes for treating
skin inflammatory diseases, such as psoriasis [4, 5]. For
example, in murine models, exosomes from human embry-
onic or umbilical cord-derived MSCs improved imiqui-
mod (IMQ)-induced psoriasis-like dermatitis by reduc-
ing keratinocyte proliferation [5]. Intradermal injection
of exosomes from human umbilical cord-derived MSCs
suppressed the proliferation of keratinocytes in an IMQ-
induced moderate psoriatic mouse model [6, 7]. However,
the exact components responsible for these effects remain
unknown.

MicroRNAs,  conserved  noncoding  RNAs  (19–25
nucleotides), regulate gene expression and are thought to
mediate exosome effects [8, 9]. Over 250 microRNAs are
differentially expressed in the skin and blood of psoria-
sis patients [10], making them potential therapeutic tar-
gets for exosome-based treatments. To investigate this,
we hypothesized that a specific microRNA from human
placenta MSC exosomes plays a key role in treating IMQ-
induced  psoriasis-like  dermatitis  in  mice.  Using  small
RNA sequencing, we identified this microRNA and tested
its effects with an inhibitor and agomiR, focusing on its
role in inflammation and downstream pathways.

Markers of MSC-exosomes, including CD63 and ALIX[14],

were detected through chemiluminescence (ECL Plus kit;
Amersham Bioscience, Buckinghamshire, UK).

Analysis of the microRNAs in MSC‑exosomes
and skin tissues of mice

For identification of the therapeutic microRNAs in the MSC-

were analyzed via small RNA sequencing [17]. To assess
microRNA expression in skin tissues, four mice received

IMQ application for 6 days resulted in an approximate 12%

were collected for small RNA sequencing.

For  sequencing,  100  ng  of  total  RNA  from  MSC-

prepared using the QIAseq® microRNA Library Kit (QIA-
GEN Inc., Hilden, German). After adaptor ligation, cDNA
synthesis, PCR amplification, and size selection (170–200
bp), libraries were assessed for quality and sequenced on the
Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina Inc., San Diego,
CA, USA), generating 75 bp single-end reads to identify
microRNAs.

Generating the inhibitor‑treated MSC‑exosomes
with specific microRNA inhibition

Our analysis identified miR-100-5p as a potential media-
tor of MSC-exosome therapeutic effects. To investigate the
role of miR-100-5p, we used a miR-100-5p inhibitor (5’-
AAC CCG UAG AUC CGA ACU UGUG-3’; Integrated DNA

performed through precipitation, resuspension, transferal,
electroporation (150 V/100 μF), removal of free-floating
microRNAs, and ultracentrifugation. The processed pellet
was stored at − 80 °C.

AgomiR‑100‑5p synthesis

The role of miR-100-5p was also investigated by employing
an agomiR-100-5p for treating IMQ-induced psoriasiform
dermatitis.  The  agomiR-100-5p  was  synthesized  (Inte-
grated DNA Technologies) with the target sequences for
agomiR-100-5p, sense: 5′ AAC CCG UAG AUC CGAA CUU
GUG  −3′, and antisense: 5′-CAC AAG UUC GGA UCU ACG
GGUU-3′.

Study design

In order to confirm the role of miR-100-5p in MSC-derived
exosome therapy for psoriasis, we randomly divided the
mice into six groups: Sham, Exo, IMQ, IMQ + Exo, IMQ
+ Exoi, and IMQ + AgomiR. Sham and Exo groups received
topical PBS for 6 days, followed by PBS or MSC-derived
exosomes (1 × 10⁸ particles per mouse) for 7 days. IMQ,
IMQ + Exo, and IMQ + Exoi groups received topical IMQ
for 6 days, followed by PBS, MSC-derived exosomes (1
× 10⁸  particles  per  mouse),  or  inhibitor-treated  MSC-
exosomes (1 × 10⁸ particles per mouse) for 7 days. The
IMQ + AgomiR group received IMQ for 6 days, followed by
intradermal agomiR-100-5p injections (2 nmol per mouse).
Mortality rates were 0% for the Sham and Exo groups, and
33.3%, 25%, 40%, and 33.3% for the IMQ, IMQ + Exo, IMQ
+ Exoi, and IMQ + Agomir groups, respectively. To ensure
6 survivors per group, the initial group sizes were 6, 6, 9, 8,

10, and 9. On the day after the final treatment, the psoriasis
severity scores, the spleen and body weight, the histological
analysis, and cytokines of skin tissues were evaluated. The
details were described in the following paragraphs.

Psoriasis area and severity index (PASI) score
of animal

PASI score of animal, including scoring of erythema, scal-
ing, and thickening (0–4 scale), was used to assess IMQ-
induced psoriatic dermatitis severity, as reported previously
[5, 6]. The cumulative score (0–12) was calculated to meas-
ure overall severity.

Euthanizing and tissue harvesting

One day after the final treatment, all surviving mice were
weighed and euthanized by decapitation. Skin tissues from
the back were harvested and then then dissected, with one
half snap-frozen and stored at −80°C, and the other half
placed in 37% formaldehyde solution (Sigma-Aldrich). The
spleen was also harvested, and the spleen/body weight ratio
was calculated.

Histological analysis

The formaldehyde-fixed skin tissue samples were embedded
in paraffin wax, serially sectioned, and then stained with
hematoxylin and eosin solution (Sigma-Aldrich). Epidermal
thickening was observed under a light microscope (Moti-
cEasyScan Pro 6; Motic Asia, Hong Kong). The thickness
of the epidermis was measured using ImageJ software (Ver-
sion 1.52e; free software by NIH, USA; available at https://
imagej. nih. gov/ ij/; accessed date: 1 August 2021).

Immunohistochemistry staining

Immunohistochemistry was used to assess Ki-67, a marker
for cell proliferation[18], in skin samples. Bioinformatic
analysis predicted mTOR as a target of miR-100-5p, so
phosphorylated  mTOR  (p-mTOR)  and  phosphorylated
S6 kinase 1 (p-S6 K1, a downstream mTOR component)
[19]  were  also  examined.  Formaldehyde-fixed,  paraffin-
embedded tissues were prepared and stained with antibodies
against Ki-67 (anti-Ki-67 antibody, IRQ 6001; iReal Tech-
nology, Hsinchu, Taiwan), phosphorylated mTOR (anti-p-
mTOR antibody, GTX132803; GeneTex, Inc, California,
USA), and phosphorylated S6 K1 (anti-p-S6 K1 antibody,
GTX635621; GeneTex). Microscopy was conducted at 200
× magnification and signal densities were quantified using
ImageJ software from six fields per section.

Results

Confirmation and biodistribution of MSC‑exosomes

MSC-exosomes were confirmed by their characteristic dou-
ble-layer, cup-shaped morphology (Fig. 1a), 100–150 nm
particle size (Fig. 1b), and positive detection of Alix and
CD63 markers (Fig. 1c). Bioluminescence imaging revealed
significantly higher Cy7-conjugated MSC-exosome signals
in the skin at 2 and 24 h post-application compared to 0 h
(both p < 0.001; Fig. 1d). Signals in other organs remained
low and unchanged over time.

Immunofluorescence staining on the day after finished
6-day IMQ and 7-day MSC-exosome or PBS treatment con-
firmed MSC-exosome distribution throughout the epidermis
in IMQ + Exo mice, and absent in IMQ-alone mice (Fig. 1e).

Identification of the target microRNA in the MSC
exosomes

Small RNA sequencing identified 981 microRNAs with sig-
nificantly different expression (p < 0.05) between the IMQ
and Sham groups. A volcano plot (Fig. 2a) shows upregu-
lated  and  downregulated  microRNAs,  while  a  heatmap
reveals 16 significantly downregulated microRNAs (with
expression < 50% of the Sham group) in IMQ-treated mice,
with high expression (> 10,000 counts) in skin tissues.

Six downregulated microRNAs in IMQ-treated skin over-
lapped with those detected in MSC-exosomes. The amount
of miR-100-5p is most abundant in the exosomes among
the six microRNAs (Fig. 2b). Additionally, bioinformatic
analysis predicted mTOR as a downstream target of miR-
100-5p (Fig. 2c) and the role of mTOR in psoriasis was well
established [20]. All of above suggested miR-100-5p might
play a role in the therapeutic effects of MSC-exosomes.

Discussion

The data from this study provide clear evidence of the ther-
apeutic effects of exosomes from human placenta MSCs
(MSC-exosomes) against psoriasis. Specifically, our find-
ings show that topical administration of MSC-exosomes
improved epidermal hyperplasia and reduced inflammatory
cytokines in mice with moderate psoriasis-like dermatitis
induced by IMQ. Given the current lack of effective ther-
apies for psoriasis, our findings have significant clinical
implications and warrant further investigation.

The composition of MSC-exosomes is complex, and the
specific components responsible for their biological activi-
ties are still being elucidated. Among the various cargo car-
ried by exosomes, microRNAs have garnered the most atten-
tion due to their crucial role in regulating immune responses
[21]. Psoriasis, on the other hand, is well known to be asso-
ciated with the dysregulation of microRNAs, which can
influence keratinocyte proliferation and differentiation pro-
cesses [22]. Based on these findings, we hypothesized that
MSC-exosomes may exert their therapeutic effects against
psoriasis by delivering specific microRNAs, particularly

titis in mice, while intradermal injection of agomiR-100-5p
replicated the therapeutic benefits of MSC-exosomes. Taken
together, these results confirm our hypothesis and demon-
strate that miR-100-5p plays a pivotal role in mediating
the therapeutic effects of human placenta MSC-exosomes
against IMQ-induced psoriasis-like dermatitis in mice.

One might wonder why MSC-exosomes from human pla-
centa exhibit significant therapeutic effects against psoria-
sis, particularly when tested in murine models. A key factor
could be the involvement of miR-100-5p, which appears to
play a pivotal role in both species. In alignment with our
findings,  previous  studies  have  demonstrated  that  miR-
100-5p is downregulated in patients with psoriasis [23].
Formerly known as miR-100, miR-100-5p belongs to the
miR-99 family, which includes miR-99a and miR-99b. This
family is among the most evolutionarily conserved micro-
RNA groups, with mature sequences that are identical in
both humans and mice [24].

This high degree of sequence conservation between spe-
cies suggests that the biological functions of miR-100-5p
are also conserved, making it an ideal candidate for inter-
species therapeutic applications. In the case of psoriasis,

which shares some pathological mechanisms between spe-
cies, MSC-exosomes from human placenta can react with
the mouse’s cellular machinery. This allows the therapeu-
tic effects observed in mice to be replicated in humans.
Thus,  the  inter-species  application  of  human  placenta
MSC-exosomes and miR-100-5p underscores the therapeu-
tic potential of these biologics not only in animal models
but also in humans. These findings lay the groundwork for
translating preclinical data into clinical therapies, offering
promising avenues for treating inflammatory diseases like
psoriasis.

To identify potential target genes of miR-100-5p in psori-
asis, we used bioinformatic predictions and literature review,
identifying mTOR as a prime candidate. Studies show that
the miR-99 family regulates mTOR signaling in squamous
cell carcinoma and wound healing [25–27]. mTOR, part of
the PI3 K/Akt pathway, influences skin cell growth, pro-
liferation, differentiation, and motility. This pathway has
gained attention as a key regulator in epidermal homeosta-
sis and inflammatory skin disorders [28]. Akt activation is
seen in psoriatic lesions, and PI3 K/Akt/mTOR also controls
immune responses linked to psoriasis [19, 20, 29]. mTOR
and S6 K1 activation in psoriatic skin indicates their involve-
ment in disease pathology. Additionally, miR-100-5p inhib-
its angiogenesis by repressing mTOR in endothelial cells.

This study has several limitations. First, intradermal
injection of agomiR-100-5p showed slightly lower thera-
peutic efficacy than topical MSC-exosomes, possibly due
to that MSC-exosomes have better cell membrane perme-
ate efficacy than naked microRNA, as MSC-exosomes can
effectively cross the stratum corneum and reach deeper
skin  layers  [30].  Other  microRNAs  in  MSC-exosomes
may also contribute to their therapeutic effect. Second,

the study used only male mice, so the effects in female
mice  with  psoriasis-like  dermatitis  remain  unknown.
Third, while exosomes are effective drug delivery systems
[31, 32], their content is typically low, potentially limiting
efficacy. Engineering exosomes to increase miR-100-5p
content could enhance their therapeutic potential for pso-
riasis. Further studies are needed before definitive insights
can be drawn.

In conclusion, miRNA-100-5p may contribute to the ther-
apeutic effects of human placenta MSC-exosomes against
IMQ-induced psoriasis in mice by modulating the mTOR/
S6 K1 pathway.
