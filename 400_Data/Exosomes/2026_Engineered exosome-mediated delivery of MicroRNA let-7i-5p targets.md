Engineered  exosomes  have  emerged  as  a  promising  platform  for  the  targeted  delivery  of  therapeutic  nucleic
acids. The microRNA let-7i-5p is recognized for its anti-inflammatory properties, but its molecular targets remain
incompletely defined. To elucidate its mechanism of action and facilitate its efficient delivery, we generated let-
7i-5p-enriched  engineered  exosomes  (EEXOs)  by  overexpressing  let-7i-5p  in  genetically  modified  RAW264.7
cells.  Droplet  digital  polymerase  chain  reaction  confirmed  that  EEXO  treatment  significantly  increased  the
intracellular level of let-7i-5p (3-fold increase; p < 0.001). Bioinformatic analyses performed using four micro-
RNA  target  prediction  databases  identified  toll-like  receptor  4  (TLR4)  as  a  potential  target.  This  finding  was
validated by a luciferase activity assay, which revealed direct binding of let-7i-5p to the 3′ untranslated region of
TLR4  mRNA,  resulting  in  its  suppression  (p  < 0.001).  In  lipopolysaccharide  (LPS)-stimulated  macrophages,
EEXO-delivered let-7i-5p downregulated TLR4 and its downstream signaling components, such as myeloid dif-
ferentiation  primary  response  88  (MyD88),  tumor  necrosis  factor  receptor–associated  factor  6  (TRAF6),  and
nuclear  factor-κB  (NF-κB),  thereby  reducing  the  levels  of  proinflammatory  cytokines  such  as  tumor  necrosis
factor-α  and interleukin-6 (all p < 0.05). In vivo, systemic EEXO administration attenuated LPS-induced liver
injury in adult male mice, as indicated by reduced histopathological damage, liver enzyme release, and tissue
edema  (all  p  < 0.05).  Furthermore,  EEXOs  suppressed  the  TLR4/MyD88/TRAF6/NF-κB  pathway  and  proin-
flammatory cytokine production in liver tissues. In conclusion, engineered exosome–delivered let-7i-5p targets
TLR4 and modulates MyD88/TRAF6/NF-κB signaling to alleviate inflammation and protect against LPS-induced
liver injury.

1. Introduction

Let-7i-5p is a small, evolutionarily conserved microRNA belonging to
the let-7 family, which plays a central role in post-transcriptional gene
regulation  [1].  MicroRNAs  such  as  let-7i-5p  bind  to  complementary
sequences  within  the  3′  untranslated  regions  (3′UTRs)  of  target
messenger  RNAs  (mRNAs),  leading  to  mRNA  degradation  or  trans-
lational repression [2]. This finely tuned regulation maintains cellular
homeostasis and balanced gene expression [3,4]. The strong sequence
conservation  of  let-7i-5p  between  humans  and  mice  underscores  its
fundamental biological importance [5].

Functionally,  let-7i-5p  participates  in  diverse  biological  processes,
including  cell  proliferation,  differentiation,  apoptosis,  immune  modu-
lation, and inflammation [1,6]. Its expression is tightly controlled dur-
ing development and in adult tissues, reflecting its broad physiological
significance [6]. Dysregulation of let-7i-5p has been linked to various
diseases, particularly cancer, where it may act as either a tumor sup-
pressor  or  an  oncogene  depending  on  cellular  context  [7].  Aberrant
expression contributes to cell-cycle imbalance, epithelial–mesenchymal
transition,  metastasis,  and  chemoresistance  [6].  Beyond  malignancy,
altered let-7i-5p levels have been reported in cardiovascular, neurode-
generative,  and  immune-mediated  disorders  [8–11],  highlighting  its
wide-ranging pathophysiological relevance.

Recent  evidence  has  underscored  the  anti-inflammatory  and  cyto-
protective  properties  of  let-7i-5p  [10–13].  Overexpression  of  this
microRNA  alleviates  inflammation,  mitochondrial  dysfunction,  and
apoptosis in hypoxia-treated cardiomyocytes [13]. Our previous studies
identified let-7i-5p as the most abundant microRNA in exosomes derived
from  human  placental  mesenchymal  stem  cells  (MSCs)  and  demon-
strated  its  critical  contribution  to  the  therapeutic  effects  of  MSC-
exosomes  against  lipopolysaccharide  (LPS)-induced  organ  injury  in
both obese and non-obese mice [10]. Moreover, engineered exosomes
enriched with let-7i-5p (EEXOs) achieved therapeutic efficacy compa-
rable to MSC-exosomes in murine sepsis models [11], underscoring the
translational potential of this microRNA.

Given that stable and targeted delivery remains a major challenge for
RNA-based  therapeutics,  engineered  exosomes  have  emerged  as  an
attractive  vehicle  for  microRNA  delivery.  Their  biocompatibility,  low
immunogenicity, and ability to traverse biological barriers confer clear
advantages  over  conventional  delivery  systems  [14–17].  Engineered
exosomes  can  be  tailored  to  encapsulate  specific  cargos—such  as
microRNAs  or  small-molecule  drugs—while  protecting  them  from
enzymatic  degradation and  enabling  precise  tissue targeting  [14–17].
These properties make them a next-generation platform for delivering
fragile nucleic acids with enhanced stability and therapeutic specificity
both in vitro and in vivo [14–17].

To  elucidate  the  molecular  mechanisms  underlying  the  anti-
inflammatory  effects  of  let-7i-5p,  this  study  employed  an  integrated
approach combining bioinformatic prediction, in vitro validation, and in
vivo  functional  assessment.  Candidate  targets  were  identified  using
multiple  microRNA  prediction  databases  and  validated  through  lucif-
erase  reporter  assays  and  gene  expression  analyses.  The  in  vivo  rele-
vance  of  these  findings  was  examined  using  a  murine  model  of  LPS-
induced liver injury. Engineered exosomes were generated by plasmid-
mediated  overexpression  of  let-7i-5p  in  RAW264.7  macrophages  to
ensure  efficient  delivery.  To  our  knowledge,  this  is  the  first  study  to
systematically  delineate  the  molecular  basis  of  let-7i-5p–mediated
inflammation control, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic modu-
lator of inflammatory disorders.

2.2. Bioinformatic analysis to predict the potential targets of let-7i-5p

Bioinformatic analyses were performed to predict the mRNA targets
of let-7i-5p by using four established algorithms: DIANA-microT 2023,
miRPATHDP,  miRTarBase,  and  MiRanda.  These  algorithms  identified
candidate genes by analyzing complementary binding sites and regula-
tory  interactions.  Raw  data  from  each  algorithm  were  refined  and
consolidated using bioinformatics tools available at MolBioTools (http
s://molbiotools.com).  To  identify  overlapping  targets,  the  results  ob-
tained  using  the  four  algorithms  were  compared,  and  shared  targets
were visualized in a Venn diagram. After the initial screening, focused
selection  was  performed  to prioritize  genes involved  in  inflammatory
pathways.  This  step  unveiled  key  let-7i-5p  targets  whose  regulation
significantly modulated immune responses and inflammation. Binding
sites of let-7i-5p within the 3′UTR sequences of predicted targets were
also identified, accounting for both Watson–Crick base pairing (|) and
G•U  wobble  base  pairing  (:)  [19].  The  analysis  indicated  oll-like

receptor 4 (TLR4) as the key target of let-7i-5p (Fig. 1), given the central
role  of  this  protein  in  the  recognition  of  pathogens,  activation  of  in-
flammatory pathways, and regulation of immune responses [20–22].

2.4.2. Effects of let-7i-5p on LPS-induced acute liver injury in mice

2.4.2.1. In vivo experimental protocols. For the in vivo experiments, we
used a well-characterized sepsis model, wherein mice were stimulated
with LPS to induce acute liver injury [10]. The model was established
through an intraperitoneal (IP) injection of LPS (25 mg/kg, E. coli O127:
B8; Sigma-Aldrich). Treatment groups were as follows: vehicle (0.9 %
NaCl) (0.5 mL, IP), vehicle (0.9 % NaCl) plus EEXOs (1 × 109 particles
per  mouse,  IP),  LPS,  LPS  plus  EEXOs,  and  LPS  plus  let-7i-5p  inhib-
itor–treated EEXOs (1 × 109 particles per mouse, IP). Two consecutive
doses of EEXOs or let-7i-5p inhibitor–treated EEXOs were administered
at 2 and 26 h after LPS or PBS injection. The mice were closely moni-
tored throughout the study.

The  LPS  dose  (25  mg/kg)  was  selected  based  on  previous  studies
demonstrating  its  ability  to  induce  substantial  injury  in  vital  organs,
including the lungs and liver [10,11]. The doses of EEXOs and let-7i-5p

5

inhibitor–treated  EEXOs  (1  × 109  particles  per  mouse)  were  likewise
chosen  according  to  earlier  findings  showing  that EEXOs  at  this  dose

3.3.1. Optimal doses of EEXOs and LPS for in vitro experiments

The viability of RAW264.7 cells treated with EEXOs (1 × 108 or 1 ×
109 particles/mL) or LPS (50 or 100 ng/mL) was similar to that of PBS-

the basis of these findings, we used the 100 ng/mL concentration of LPS
in subsequent experiments.

To determine the optimal EEXO dose for the in vitro experiments,
RAW264.7 cells were treated with PBS, LPS (100 ng/mL), or LPS plus

particles/mL than in those treated with LPS alone (p = 0.010; Fig. 3B).
However, no significant difference in IL-6 level was observed between
cells treated with  LPS plus  EEXOs at  1 × 108  particles/mL  and  those
treated with LPS alone (p = 0.304; Fig. 3B). These findings confirmed the
anti-inflammatory  effect  of  EEXOs  at  1  × 109  particles/mL  in  LPS-

Viability  was  similar  between  cells  treated  with  LPS  (100  ng/mL)
alone and those treated with LPS plus EEXOs (1 × 109 particles/mL; p =
0.473; Fig. 3C). These findings confirmed that EEXOs at 1 × 109 parti-
cles/mL did not adversely affect cell viability, regardless of LPS stimu-
lation. On the basis of these results, the 1 × 109  particles/mL dose of
EEXOs was used in subsequent in vitro experiments.

4. Discussion

4.1. Therapeutic potential of let-7i-5p delivery via engineered exosomes

To  enable  efficient  delivery  of  let-7i-5p,  we  prepared  engineered
exosomes by genetically modifying RAW264.7 cells to overexpress this
microRNA. Engineered exosomes enriched with therapeutic microRNAs,
such as let-7i-5p, represent a novel and promising strategy for the tar-
geted treatment of inflammatory diseases. As naturally secreted nano-
vesicles, exosomes possess several intrinsic advantages pertaining to the
delivery  of  small  molecules—for  example,  they  are  biocompatible,

nonimmunogenic, and capable of crossing biological barriers [14–17]..
Thus, engineered exosomes carrying therapeutic microRNAs provide a
cell-free,  precision-targeted  approach  with  considerable  advantages
over conventional gene- or drug-based therapies [14–17]..

The  present  study  demonstrated  that  genetic  modification  of
RAW264.7  cells  to  overexpress  let-7i-5p  significantly  increased  the
therapeutic  content  of  engineered  exosomes,  resulting  in  an  approxi-
mately 11-fold increase in let-7i-5p concentration compared with that in
BEXOs. The engineered exosomes preserved key exosomal characteris-
tics, such as morphology, particle size distribution, and classical markers
(CD9, CD63, and ALIX) expression, which confirmed their integrity and
suitability  for  therapeutic  use.  EEXOs were  determined  to  be  safe  for
RAW264.7  cells.  The  uptake  of  EEXOs  by  these  cells  resulted  in  an
approximately 3-fold increase in the intracellular concentration of let-
7i-5p, demonstrating efficient delivery. The increase let-7i-5p concen-
tration significantly alleviated inflammation both in vitro and in vivo.
Collectively, these findings highlight engineered exosome–mediated let-
7i-5p delivery as a promising therapeutic approach for inflammation-
related diseases, such as sepsis.

Our findings complement and extend recent advances in engineered
exosome–microRNA therapies for inflammatory diseases. For example, a
study  demonstrated the  efficacy  of Wharton’s  jelly MSC–derived  exo-

These exosomes improved lung function and reduced collagen deposi-

marily through antifibrotic and immunomodulatory mechanisms, such

4.3. Mechanistic insights into TLR4 mRNA dynamics

Activation of TLR4 by LPS initiates a tightly orchestrated signaling
cascade  that  coordinates  the  innate  immune  response.  Upon  recogni-
tion, LPS binds to LPS-binding protein (LBP) and is transferred to the
TLR4 receptor complex via the co-receptor CD14 [20]. Myeloid differ-
entiation factor 2 (MD-2) facilitates the dimerization of TLR4/MD-2, a
key step that triggers receptor internalization through endocytosis [46].
This dimerization event activates the MyD88/TRAF6/NF-κB pathway,
leading to the production of proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α
and  IL-6  [47].  As  endocytosis  proceeds,  MyD88-dependent  NF-κB
signaling  is  downregulated  [47,48],  and  internalized  receptors  are
directed either toward lysosomal degradation or recycling to the plasma
membrane  [49].  NF-κB  can  further  enhance  TLR4  transcription,
replenishing receptor levels and maintaining signaling capacity [38].

Beyond  transcriptional  regulation,  posttranscriptional  control  of
TLR4 mRNA stability represents another layer of immune modulation.
The  3′UTR  of  TLR4  contains  AU-rich  elements  (AREs),  rendering  the
transcript inherently unstable and susceptible to degradation by ARE-
binding  proteins  [50].  During  LPS  stimulation,  however,  stabilizing
factors such as HuR can bind these regions to protect the transcript from
decay,  thereby  transiently  sustaining  receptor  expression  [51].  This
interplay between degradation and stabilization enables a rapid but self-

limiting inflammatory response.

Consistent  with  these  mechanisms,  our  results  showed  that  TLR4
mRNA expression peaked at 12 h following LPS exposure, with no sig-
nificant changes at 6 or 24 h (Fig. 5C). This transient elevation likely
reflects the combined influence of NF-κB–driven transcriptional activa-
tion and HuR-mediated mRNA stabilization, followed by compensatory
receptor internalization, degradation, and recycling. The short half-life
of TLR4 mRNA further contributes to this transient expression pattern,
suggesting that the 12-h peak represents a critical regulatory juncture
within the self-limiting dynamics of TLR4 signaling [49–51].

We further identified let-7i-5p as a direct posttranscriptional regu-
lator of TLR4. Luciferase reporter assays confirmed specific binding of
let-7i-5p  to  the  TLR4  3′UTR,  resulting  in  reduced  reporter  activity,
which was abolished when the binding site was mutated. Functionally,
overexpression of let-7i-5p suppressed LPS-induced TLR4 mRNA upre-
gulation,  likely  by  promoting  transcript  degradation.  These  findings
suggest that let-7i-5p counteracts the stabilizing effect of LPS-activated
RNA-binding  proteins  such  as  HuR,  restoring  physiological  mRNA
turnover and preventing sustained receptor accumulation.

The  downstream  consequences  of  this  regulation  were  evident  in
both in vitro and in vivo systems. In LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 cells, let-
7i-5p  reduced  TLR4  expression,  suppressed  MyD88/TRAF6/NF-κB
activation, and diminished TNF-α and IL-6 secretion. In mouse models,
administration  of  let-7i-5p–enriched  EEXOs  markedly  alleviated  LPS-
induced  acute  liver  injury,  as  reflected  by  lower  serum  AST  and  ALT
levels  and  reduced  liver  wet-to-dry  ratios,  indicating  attenuated
inflammation and improved organ integrity. Conversely, inhibition of
let-7i-5p  exacerbated  tissue  injury,  accompanied  by  elevated  TLR4
expression,  enhanced  NF-κB  activation,  and
increased  cytokine
production.

Taken  together,  these  findings  identify  let-7i-5p  as  a  key  negative
feedback  regulator  of  TLR4  signaling.  By  promoting  degradation  of
TLR4 mRNA and dampening NF-κB–mediated cytokine expression, let-
7i-5p  restrains  excessive  inflammation  and  protects  against  tissue
injury.  These  results  highlight  the  therapeutic  potential  of  let-7i-
5p–based interventions for inflammatory disorders driven by aberrant
TLR4 activation.

4.4. Advantages of using a nonstem cell source for exosome production

A key strength of our approach is the use of a nonstem cell source for
exosome  production.  Although  MSC-derived  exosomes  have  attracted
considerable attention for their therapeutic potential, concerns remain
regarding  batch-to-batch  variability,  scalability,  and  potential  onco-
genic risks associated with the use of stem cell sources [52]. By contrast,
the use of a well-characterized and stable cell line such as RAW264.7
provides  several  practical  advantages:  ease  of  genetic  manipulation,
reproducible exosome yield and cargo content, and fewer ethical and
regulatory  challenges  than  those  with  stem  cell–derived  products.  In
addition, macrophage-derived exosomes may confer further benefits in
inflammatory  contexts  because  of  their  intrinsic  homing  capacity  to
inflamed  tissues  [53].  Collectively,  these  attributes  make  RAW264.7
macrophage–derived engineered exosomes a scalable, controllable, and
safe  platform  for  microRNA  delivery,  supporting  their  translational
potential for treating inflammation-associated diseases.

4.5. Translational significance and clinical implications

At  the  clinical  level,  TLR4  has  long  been  considered  a  promising
therapeutic target in sepsis and related inflammatory diseases; however,
direct pharmacologic inhibition has yielded disappointing results. Eri-
toran (E5564), a synthetic lipid A analog that competitively blocks LPS
binding  to  the  TLR4/MD-2  complex,  was  evaluated  in  the  Phase  III
ACCESS trial but failed to reduce 28-day mortality in nearly 2000 pa-
tients  with  sepsis  [54].  Similarly,  TAK-242  (Resatorvid),  a  small-
molecule  cyclohexene  derivative  that  binds  to  the  intracellular  TIR

Exosomal delivery of let-7i-5p offers a relatively refined strategy by
selectively modulating TLR4 expression at the posttranscriptional level

In addition to facilitating the development of sepsis, TLR4 activation
contributes  to  the  pathogenesis  of  several  chronic  inflammatory  dis-

5p  may  extend  to  a  broader  spectrum  of  conditions  characterized  by
aberrant TLR4 signaling. Supporting this concept, studies have reported
that  placenta  MSC–derived  exosomes  exert  protective  effects  in  these
settings, with let-7 family members emerging as key mediators. Further
research is required to establish the efficacy, durability, and safety of
engineered exosomal let-7i-5p therapy across clinical contexts.

4.6. Study limitations

This study has several limitations. First, we investigated the effects of
EEXO-mediated let-7i-5p delivery in a short timeframe—24 h in vitro
(RAW264.7 cells) and 48 h in vivo (acute liver injury model). Longer-
term studies are required to determine the durability of therapeutic ef-
ficacy  and  potential  toxicity  of  EEXOs.  In  addition,  we  tested  only  a
single  therapeutic  protocol  for  mitigating  LPS-induced  liver  injury  in
mice. Whether alternative EEXO dosing regimens or injection intervals
would  yield  similar  results  remains  unclear.  The  present  protocol,
adapted from our previous study [11], involved the IP administration of
two consecutive doses of EEXOs (1 × 109 particles per mouse, every 24
h). This approach significantly mitigated LPS-induced liver injury. Our
previous study revealed that the in vivo half-life of EEXOs is approxi-
mately 48 h [11], suggesting that a single dose of 1 × 109 particles per
mouse could exert therapeutic effects. Further studies are necessary to
confirm these possibilities.

Second, although the therapeutic effects of EEXO-delivered let-7i-5p
were demonstrated in cell-based models and in male mice, its efficacy in
female mice and humans remains unknown. Given the well-documented
sex-based differences in immune responses [58,59], the magnitude or
durability of let-7i-5p-mediated TLR4 suppression may differ between
the two sexes. Estrogen and testosterone modulate TLR4 expression and
NF-κB signaling [60,61], which are central to the mechanistic pathways
identified  in  this  study.  Emerging  evidence  further  suggests  that  bio-
logical  sex  can  influence  exosome  pharmacology  [62,63].  In  mice,
intravenously  administered  exosomes  exhibit  sex-dependent  bio-
distribution,  with  males  showing  greater  accumulation  in  the  liver,
kidney, and brain, and females showing higher uptake in the lungs [62].
Studies  of  nanoparticle  pharmacokinetics  indicate  that  sex  hormones
can alter immune  clearance, vascular  permeability, and  tissue uptake
[63],  providing  a  mechanistic  rationale  for  potential  sex-dependent
differences  in  exosome  biodistribution.  Moreover,  preclinical  studies
have reported sex-dependent differences in the therapeutic efficacy of
exosome-based interventions [64,65], further supporting this concept.
As  this  study  used  only  male  mice,  sex-related  variations  in  the  bio-
distribution and therapeutic efficacy of EEXO-delivered let-7i-5p cannot
be  excluded,  and  future  investigations  in  female  animals  and  sex-
stratified human cohorts are warranted.

Third,  we  focused  exclusively  on  let-7i-5p,  without  comparing  its
effects to other microRNAs that confer protection in inflammatory and
septic  models.  For  instance,  miR-146a  and  miR-21  suppress  NF-κB

signaling  [43,44],  and  miR-206-3p  mitigates  LPS-induced  acute  lung
injury  by  inhibiting  the  TLR4/nucleotide-binding  oligomerization
domain leucine-rich repeat and pyrin domain containing 3 axis [45]. A
head-to-head  comparison  between  let-7i-5p  and  other  microRNAs
would  clarify  their  relative  efficacy  and  therapeutic  potential  for
inflammation-induced organ injury.

Finally, although TLR4 emerged as a direct target of let-7i-5p, we did
not comprehensively explore other pathways regulated by this micro-
RNA. Members of the let-7 family modulate a wide range of immune
mediators,  and  a  study  revealed  their  involvement  in  the  following
pathways: mitogen-activated protein kinase/activating protein-1, TIR-
domain-containing adapter-inducing interferon-β/interferon regulatory
factor  3,  and  cytokine  receptor  signaling  cascades  [66].  Moreover,
beyond  TLR4,  bioinformatic  analyses  predict  that  let-7i-5p  may  also
regulate multiple downstream effectors. For instance, miRPathDB and
miRTarBase  identify  high  mobility  group  box  1  (HMGB1),  signal
transducer  and  activator  of  transcription  3  (STAT3),  suppressor  of
cytokine signaling 1 (SOCS1), and interleukin-1 receptor-associated ki-
nase 1 (IRAK1) as potential targets of let-7i-5p (data not shown). These
molecules  play  pivotal  roles  in  inflammation,  fibrosis,  and  immune
regulation [67–70], and dysregulation of their signaling pathways has
been implicated in sepsis, acute and chronic lung injury, liver fibrosis,
and cancer-related inflammation [67–74]. As this study did not evaluate
the effects of let-7i-5p on these pathways, potential parallel or syner-
gistic  mechanisms  cannot  be  excluded.  Future  studies  integrating  un-
biased  transcriptomic  and  proteomic  profiling,  followed  by  pathway-
specific  validation,  are  warranted  to  delineate  these  interactions  and
strengthen the mechanistic understanding of let-7i-5p-mediated thera-
peutic effects.

5. Conclusion

We generated and rigorously characterized EEXOs, which retained
canonical exosomal features and had an approximately 11-fold higher
let-7i-5p concentration than did BEXOs. EEXOs were efficiently inter-
nalized by RAW264.7 cells, increasing the intracellular concentration of
let-7i-5p  by  approximately  3-fold  without  affecting  viability.  Mecha-

effects, supporting an on-target mechanism.

Our findings should be interpreted with caution because the present
study was limited to short observation periods (24 h in vitro and 48 h in
vivo),  male  mice,  and  a  single  dosing  regimen.  Mechanistic  analyses
focused only on the MyD88/NF-κB axis, without assessing other relevant
pathways. Furthermore, no direct comparisons were made with other
microRNAs. Future studies should optimize dosing and delivery; eval-
uate  pharmacokinetics,  biodistribution,  durability,  and  safety;  and
extend investigations to female animals and additional sepsis and organ
injury  models.  Broader  mechanistic  analyses  and  comparative  or
scalable

the  EEXO  platform  toward  robust  preclinical  validation  and  eventual
clinical translation for TLR4-associated inflammatory diseases.
