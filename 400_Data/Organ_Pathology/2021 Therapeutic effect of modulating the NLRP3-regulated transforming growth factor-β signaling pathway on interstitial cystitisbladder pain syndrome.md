Interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome (IC/BPS) is a disorder with complex pathogenesis and lacks effective
treatment.  Chronic  inflammation  is  the  main  pathogenesis  of  Hunner-type  IC/BPS.  The  NLR  family  pyrin
domain-containing  3  (NLRP3)  inflammasome-related  transforming  growth  factor-β  (TGF-β)/Smad  signaling
pathway  plays  a  crucial  role  in  inflammation-related  tissue  fibrosis.  Lipopolysaccharide  (LPS)  and  protamine
sulfate  (LPS/PS)  were  instilled  into  the  mouse  bladder  twice  a  week  for  5  consecutive  weeks  to  establish  a
chronic inflammation-induced IC/BPS model (LPS/PS model). Following LPS/PS treatment, curcumin (oral, 100
mg/kg; a potent NLRP3 modulator) was administered for 2 weeks in the curcumin treatment group, and normal
saline was used for the sham group. Bladder function was evaluated by performing the voiding spot assay and
examining the status of urothelial denudation and fibrosis in bladder tissues. The expression of NLRP3 inflam-
masome,  interleukin-1β,  TGF-β,  Smad,  vimentin,  and  E-cadherin  in  bladder  tissues  was  evaluated  through
immunohistochemistry  staining.  Results  revealed  that  the  repeated  instillation  of  LPS/PS  leads  to  voiding
dysfunction,  bladder  urothelium  denudation,  and  detrusor  muscle  fibrosis  through  the  upregulation  of  the
NLRP3 inflammasome/IL-1β-related TGF-β/Smad pathway and the increased epithelial–mesenchymal transition
process in bladder tissues. The downregulation of the NLRP3 inflammasome/IL-1β-related TGF-β/Smad pathway
in bladder tissues through curcumin effectively mitigated bladder injury in the LPS/PS model. In conclusion, the
NLRP3 inflammasome/IL-1β-related TGF-β/Smad pathway plays a crucial role in bladder injury in the LPS/PS
model,  and  modulation  of  this  pathway,  such  as  by  using  curcumin,  can  effectively  mitigate  the  sequelae  of
chronic inflammation-induced IC/BPS.

1. Introduction

Interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome (IC/BPS) is a troublesome
disease presenting with irritative voiding symptoms and pelvic pain and
is associated with medical comorbidities such as depression and anxiety
[1,2]. A high misdiagnosis rate and lack of effective treatment severely
affect patients’  quality of life because the precise etiology and patho-
physiology of IC/BPS are not elucidated [3–5]. Several etiological the-
ories have been proposed on the initiation of bladder injury in patients
with IC/PBS [5,6]. In Hunner-type IC/BPS patients, the main histolog-

Although multiple treatment options are available for IC/BPS, IC/
BPS patients have higher health care costs and comorbidities than non-
IC/BPS patients due to lack effective and lasting treatment options [18].
An effective therapy with a high safety profile and easy-to-use regimen is
urgently needed to increase medication adherence and alleviate symp-
toms  in  IC/BPS  patients.  Curcumin  is  a  polyphenol  derived  from
turmeric (Curcuma longa) rhizome, which is commonly used as a spice
and herb in Asia [19]. Curcumin is used as a potential therapeutic agent
for several chronic diseases, especially inflammatory diseases, because
of its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory proper-
ties [19]. The NLRP3/IL-1β  pathway could be effectively inhibited by
curcumin,  and  curcumin  has  great  potential  to  treat  NLRP3-induced
diseases  [20,21].  In  animal  studies,  curcumin  has  been  effectively
used to treat myocardial fibrosis, liver cirrhosis, and idiopathic pulmo-
nary  fibrosis  [21–23].  However,
the  effect  of  curcumin  on
inflammation-induced  bladder  injury  has  not  been  studied.  We  hy-
pothesized  that  the  downregulation  of  NLRP3  expression  in  bladder
tissues  by  curcumin  can  attenuate  bladder  injury  in  Hunner-type
IC/BPS. Thus, we evaluated the therapeutic effect of curcumin and its
possible  therapeutic  mechanisms  in  inflammation-induced  bladder
injury disease by using a bladder-centric mouse model.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Animals

In this study, we used young adult female BALB/c mice (22–26 g in
weight; BioLASCO, Taipei, Taiwan). This animal study was approved by
the  Institutional  Animal  Care  and  Use  Committee  of  Taipei  Medical
University  (LAC-2019-0248).  The  care  and  handling  of  mice  were  in
accordance with the guidelines of the National Institutes of Health, the
United States.

2.2. Chronic inflammation-induced IC/BPS mouse model
(lipopolysaccharide/protamine sulfate model) and study design

Eight-week-old female BALB/c mice were anesthetized with zoletil/
xylazine  (40/10  mg/kg,  i.p.),  and  urethral  catheterization  was  per-
formed using a 24-gauge angiocatheter to empty the bladder and instill
drugs. Subsequently, mice were treated with protamine sulfate (PS) and
lipopolysaccharide  (LPS).  First,  PS  (1.5  mg/mice,  Sigma-Aldrich,  St.
Louis, MO, USA) was instilled into the bladder and kept in the bladder
for 30 min. After draining out PS, the bladder was washed with normal
saline and then emptied. Then, LPS (150 μg/mouse, Sigma-Aldrich, St.
Louis, MO, USA) was instilled into the bladder and kept in the bladder
for  30  min.  To  increase  the  urothelial  permeability  and  enhance  the
inflammatory injury of the bladder induced by LPS, PS was used before
LPS instillation. PS- and LPS-treated mice received PS and LPS bladder
instillation twice weekly (day 1 and day 4 per week) for 5 consecutive
weeks. In the sham group, an equal volume of normal saline (100 μL) as
the volume of PS combined with LPS was used. In the treatment group,
100  mg/kg  curcumin  (ChemCruz,  Huissen,  the  Netherlands;  oral
gavage) was administered for 5 consecutive days, and no treatment was
administered  for  the  next  2  days.  The  regimen  was  followed  for  2
consecutive weeks.

To  confirm  the  functional  and  histological  results  of  the  LPS/PS
model  in  mice,  PS  with  LPS  treatment  (LPS  group,  n  = 3)  and  sham
procedure (sham group, n = 3) were used. In the following formal study,

curcumin  alone  (Cur  group)  and  LPS  plus  curcumin  (LC  group)  were
added. In total, four groups (n = 3 in sham and Cur groups and n = 6 in
LPS and LC groups) were used to evaluate the role of NLRP3-induced
fibrosis in the LPS/PS model (Fig. 1).

2.3. Micturition assessment

After  complete  treatment,  the  void  spot  assay  was  performed  to
evaluate the micturition pattern of mice as described previously [24].
Mice (one mouse per cage) were placed in a cage containing standard
rodent  chow  but  no  water  and  lined  with  0.35-mm  filter  paper
(26.5 × 21 cm; No. GFP001; GenePure, Taichung, Taiwan) for 4 h in a
dark room. Urine spots on filter papers were imaged using a UVP UVsolo
touch darkroom ultraviolet imaging cabinet (Analytik, Jena, Germany).
Image capture settings were adjusted using the UVP VisionWorks touch
image  acquisition  software.  Images  were  captured  by  performing  the
ninhydrin  test  (1%  ninhydrin  in  75%  EtOH  solution)  and  through
365-nm UV illumination. Exposure settings were optimized to maximize
the  signal  over  noise.  The  numbers  and  areas  of  urine  spots  were
recorded.

The bladder capacity was measured as the amount of the maximal
instilled normal saline volume. A 24-gauge angiocatheter was inserted
into the bladder, and the bladder was infused with normal saline at a
rate  of  0.2 mL/min.  The  infusion  was  stopped  when  urine  leakage
occurred,  and  the  total  infusion  volume  was  recorded  as  the  bladder
capacity.

2.4. Evaluation of histologic changes

The bladder urothelium integrity was evaluated through the immu-
nohistochemistry (IHC) staining of cytokeratin. The 5-μm paraffin sec-
tions  of  bladder tissues  were  incubated  with  the  primary  antibody  of
cytokeratin  (dilution;  ab9377,  Abcam,  Cambridge,  UK).  The  staining
procedures were conducted using a Ventana Benchmark XT autostainer
(Ventana  Medical  Systems,  Tucson,  AZ,  USA)  according  to  manufac-
turer’s instructions. All sections were observed under the TissueGnostics
Axio Observer Z1 microscope (TissueGnostics GmbH, Vienna, Austria).
The bladder tissues were stained with Masson’s trichrome stain by
using the Trichrome Stain Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) to
assess the fibrosis status as described previously [25]. Random six fields
from each sample in three different layers (urothelium, lamina propria,
and muscle) were selected to quantify the density of Masson’s trichrome
staining,  and  the  density  was  examined  using  the  ImageJ  software,
version 1.52e. The average value of the staining density of six fields in
each section was recorded.

2.5. Evaluation expression of NLRP3 inflammasome and IL-1β in bladder
tissues

The  expression  of  the  inflammasome  components  NLRP3  and
apoptosis-associated  speck-like  protein  containing  a  caspase  recruit-
ment domain (ASC) and IL-1β in bladder tissues was observed through
IHC staining to evaluate the inflammatory status of bladder tissues. The
5-μm paraffin sections of bladder tissues were incubated with the pri-
mary  antibodies  of  NLRP3  (dilution  1:200;  ab214185,  Abcam,  Cam-
bridge,  UK),  ASC  (dilution  1:200;  NBP1-78977,  Novus  Biologicals,
Littleton, CO, USA), and IL-1β (dilution 1:50; AF-401-NA, R&D Systems,
Minneapolis, MN, USA) as described previously [26]. Random six fields
from each sample in three different layers (bladder urothelium, lamina
propria,  and  muscle)  were  selected  to  quantify  the  density  by  using
ImageJ software, version 1.52e. The average value of the staining den-
sity of six fields in each section was recorded.

2.7. Immunoblotting assay

For  detection  of  NLRP3  inflammasome/IL-1β-related  TGF-β/Smad
pathway and EMT relative proteins in bladder tissues, immunoblotting
assay  was  performed.  Snap  frozen  bladder  tissues  were  thawed,  ho-
mogenized,  microwaved,  centrifuged  and  then  the  supernatants  were
collected  for  analyses.  Protein  concentration  of  each  sample  was
measured  (bicinchoninic  acid  protein  assay  kit,  Pierce  Chemical  Co.,
Rockford, IL, USA). Equal amount proteins were separated by electro-
phoresis  and  then  transferred  to  nitrocellulose  membranes  (Bio-Rad
Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA). Then, the membranes were incubated
overnight with primary antibody of NLRP3 inflammasome/IL-1β-related
TGF-β/Smad pathway-related proteins (including NLRP3, IL-1β, TGF-β1,
p-Smad2,  p-Smad3),  EMT-related  proteins  (vimentin,  E-cadherin),  or
actin  (as  the  internal  standard;  Sigma-Aldrich,  St.  Louis,  MO,  USA).
Bound  antibody  was  detected  by  chemiluminescence  (ECL  plus  kit;
Amersham  BioSciences,  Buckinghamshire,  UK).  Experiments  were
repeated three times (Supplementary Fig. 1). The images were acquired
by multispectral imaging system (UVP BioSpectrum 815 Imaging Sys-
tem) and analyzed by Vision Works LS software (UVP, California, USA).

2.8. Statistical analysis

Between-group differences were analyzed using the unpaired t-test,
one-way analysis of variance, and post hoc pairwise comparisons with
Tukey’s test. Statistical significance was indicated if p < 0.05. The prism
package in GraphPad (GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA) was
used for statistical analyses.

3.1. Chronic inflammation-induced bladder injury and voiding
dysfunction in LPS/PS mouse model

The  spontaneous  void  spot  assay  was  used  to  quantify  the  rodent
voiding  function.  Our  results  revealed  a  significant  increase  in  the
number of urine spots in the LPS group compared to the sham group
(p = 0.0001;  Fig.  2A).  The  urine  spot  area  of  each  micturition
(p = 0.0005) was significantly decreased in the LPS group compared to
the  sham  group  (Fig.  2A).  Furthermore,  the  bladder  capacity  was
significantly decreased in the LPS group compared to the sham group
(p = 0.0065;  Fig.  2B).  The  results  of  the  voiding  function  assay
demonstrated that inducing chronic inflammation using LPS/PS signif-
icantly exacerbated storage symptoms because of a decreased bladder
capacity.

The bladder urothelial defect plays a crucial role in irritative voiding
symptoms.  Cytokeratin  IHC  was  used  to  evaluate  the  integrity  of  the
bladder  urothelium.  After  LPS/PS  treatment  for  5  consecutive  weeks,
the defect of the bladder urothelium in a broad area was observed in the
LPS  group.  A  decreased  bladder  capacity  with  an  increased  urinary
frequency  was  related  to  the  results  of  fibrosis-induced  poor  bladder
compliance [29]. We further evaluated the fibrosis status of the bladder
wall with Masson’s trichrome staining. Strong staining of submucosal
and muscle layers was observed in the LPS group, and light staining was
noted in the sham group. The intensities of Masson’s trichrome staining
in submucosal and muscle layers were significantly higher in the LPS
group  compared  to  the  sham  group  (p < 0.0001  in  submucosal  and
muscle layers; Fig. 2C). In summary, the typical histological findings of
chronic  inflammation-induced  IC/BPS,  such  as  bladder  urothelial
denudation  and  bladder  wall  fibrosis,  were  observed  in  the  LPS/PS
model.

4. Discussion

The etiology of IC/BPS remains complex and uncertain, and multiple
animal models have been developed to evaluate the disease pathogen-
esis and develop a novel treatment [4,33]. However, none of the animal
models  could  duplicate  the  disease  course  in  humans  [33].  Bladder
chronic  inflammation  has  been  identified  as  the  main  player  in  the
pathogenesis of Hunner-type IC/BPS, and bladder-centric models play a
crucial role in evaluating possible pathogenesis and testing the thera-
peutic  effect  of  medications  [8,33].  A  simple  and  reproducible

The therapeutic effect of curcumin on chronic inflammation-induced

IC/BPS  mice  was  supported  by  the  attenuation  of  irritative  voiding

our study, oral curcumin administration for 2 consecutive weeks showed
a  favorable  therapeutic  potential  for  Hunner-type  IC/BPS  patients
because of the easy-to-use regimen and the safety profile of curcumin
[36]. In line with the results of this study, the treatment efficacy of orally
administered curcumin for inflammation-related diseases, such as dia-

anti-inflammatory,  antioxidative,  and

play a crucial role in the chronic inflammation-induced IC/BPS model
[41,42]. Additional studies are needed to determine the expression of
curcumin  metabolites  in  the  urine  and  bladder  tissues  of  the
inflammation-induced IC/BPS mice. Furthermore, according to the re-
sults  of  this  study,  targeting  the  NLRP3  inflammasome/IL-1β-related
TGF-β/Smad  signaling  pathway  has  a  great  therapeutic  potential  for
patients with chronic inflammation-induced IC/BPS. Novel and precise

Our study provided the following crucial results: first, consecutive
instillation  of  LPS  and  PS  in  the  bladder  for  5  weeks  established  a
chronic  inflammation-induced  IC/BPS  animal  model;  second,  NLRP3
inflammasome  activation  initiated  bladder  injury  through  the  TGF-
β/Smad  pathway,  which  plays  a  crucial  role  in  the  pathogenesis  of
chronic inflammation-induced IC/BPS; third, modulation of the NLRP3
inflammasome/IL-1β-related TGF-β/Smad pathway by curcumin can be
an  effective  therapy  for  chronic  inflammation-induced  IC/BPS.  How-
ever, this study has some limitations. First, the effect on pain manage-
ment  of  curcumin  could  not  be  assessed  because  pain  score  was  not
recorded in this study. Second, the effects of curcumin on the urethra or
other visceral organs were not evaluated in this study. Third, the results
of  this  study  are  focused  on  chronic  inflammation-induced  IC/BPS
(Hunner-type  IC/BPS)  by  using  bladder-centric  model.  Other  animal
models, such as psychological stress model should be used to evaluate
other  etiologies  related  to  IC/BPS.  Fourth,  the  translation  value  of
experimental  results  into  humans  is  still  limited  because  of  the  com-
plexities of pathogenesis in IC/BPS patients. However, the animal model
is  still  important  to  evaluate  the  pathogenesis  of  this  disease  and  to
search for novel treatment targets for treating this disorder.

5. Conclusion

The pathogenesis of chronic inflammation-induced IC/BPS is asso-
ciated  with  the  NLRP3  inflammasome/IL-1β-related  TGF-β/Smad
pathway.  Downregulation  of  the  expression  of  the  NLRP3  inflamma-
some/IL-1β-related TGF-β/Smad pathway through curcumin mitigates
chronic inflammation-induced bladder injury.
