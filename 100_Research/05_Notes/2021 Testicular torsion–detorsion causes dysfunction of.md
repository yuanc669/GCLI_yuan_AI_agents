Abstract

Background: Semen quality impairment is a serious consequence of testicular torsion–

detorsion. Adequate germ-cell mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation plays a crucial

role in male fertility. Changes in cellular oxidative phosphorylation in testicular tissues

after testicular torsion–detorsion remain unclear.

Objectives: This study investigated whether testicular torsion–detorsion induces

alternations of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation in testicular tissues.

Materials and Methods: BALB/c male mice were divided into a Sham group and

a testicular torsion–detorsion group. At the end of the procedure, the mice were

euthanized, and their bilateral testicles were removed. Mitochondria morphology

was evaluated through transmission electron microscopy. The cellular respiratory

functions of germ cells were evaluated using a Seahorse analyzer assay. The proteome

profiles in testicular tissues were analyzed using liquid chromatography–tandem

mass spectrometry. The differences in the expression levels of each component in the

oxidative phosphorylation were revealed using Ingenuity Pathways Analysis.

Results: Inner mitochondrial membrane disruption was found in ipsilateral twisted tes-

ticular mitochondria in the torsion–detorsion group but not in contralateral untwisted

testes. The cellular respiratory function in germ cells was significantly decreased

after testicular torsion–detorsion in ipsilateral twisted testes but not in contralat-

eral untwisted testes. Liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry analysis

the detorsion group, the left testis was untwisted to its natural position.

The sham operation included similar surgical procedures except that

the left testis remained untwisted. Bilateral testes were removed after

24 h of detorsion or sham procedure. The mice were divided into the

through transmission electron microscopy

The testicular tissues were processed as previously described.18 The

sections of bilateral testes in each group were examined using a trans-

mission electron microscope (Hitachi HT-7700; Hitachi, Ltd., Tokyo,

Japan). The morphologies of mitochondria in testicular tissues and

spermatozoa were evaluated; as previously described, mitochondrial

structural deformity was defined as being either deformation of the

Five random fields (2 µm2) were selected for counting the number of

2.5

LC–MS/MS analysis

Protein solutions (10 µg) of testicular tissues in ipsilateral testes of

TD and Sham groups were diluted and digested with sequencing-grade
modified porcine trypsin (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) at 37◦C for
16 h. Peptides were desalted and dried through vacuum centrifuga-
tion, and they were stored at a temperature of –80◦C until use. The
digested peptides were diluted in high-performance liquid chromatog-

raphy (HPLC) buffer A (0.1% formic acid; Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, MO,

USA), loaded into a reversed-phase pre-column (Zorbax 300SB-C18,
0.3 × 5 mm2; Agilent Technologies, Wilmington, DE, USA), and then
separated on a homemade column (HydroRP 2.5 µm, 75 µm I.D. × 20 cm
with a 15-µm tip) using a multistep gradient of HPLC buffer B (99.9%

acetonitrile/0.1% formic acid) for 70 min. The liquid chromatography

apparatus (Dionex RSLC system; Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose,
CA, USA) with electrospray interface was coupled with Orbitrap Elite™

Hybrid Ion Trap-Orbitrap Mass Spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scien-

tific) operated using Xcalibur 2.2 software (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Mass spectrometry (MS) was operated in data-dependent acquisition

mode of which internal calibration was performed followed by one full

MS scan and 20 tandem MS (MS/MS) scan events for the 20 most abun-

dant precursor ions in the full MS scan. The automatic gain control was
set as maximum accumulated time of 100 ms or ion counts of 3 × 106
ions for full MS scan and 200 ms or 3 × 103 ions for MS/MS scans.

hits were removed.

To estimate comparative protein abundance in the testis tissues,

label-free semiquantitation of protein levels was determined by using
spectral counting method.20 The normalized spectral count of each

protein in the sample was calculated by dividing the spectral count of a

given protein by the total spectral counts of the sample, and then multi-

plying the average total spectral count of each sample. The fold change

of normalized spectral count of each protein in TD group relative to

Sham group was recorded.

2.7

Functional enrichment analysis by using IPA

The differentially expressed proteins with a fold change higher or lower

than 1.5 in TD group compared to Sham group were selected for func-

tional enrichment analysis by using IPA (Qiagen Inc., Valencia, CA,

USA). The enriched canonical pathways in the ipsilateral testes of TD

and Sham groups were verified using the right-tailed Fisher’s exact

test and presented as the negative logarithm of the unadjusted p-value
(−log(p)). The pathway was considered significant if the −log(p) > 1.3
(p < 0.05). The z-score represented the activation states (upregulated

or downregulated) of canonical pathways.

2.8

Immunoblotting assay

For validation of the downregulated proteins in OXPHOS com-

plexes in ipsilateral testicular tissues, immunoblotting assay was per-

formed. Snap frozen testicular tissues were thawed, homogenized,

microwaved, and centrifuged and then the supernatants were col-

lected. Protein concentration of each sample was measured (bicin-

choninic acid protein assay kit; Pierce Chemical Co., Rockford, IL, USA).

Equal amount of proteins was separated by electrophoresis and then

transferred to nitrocellulose membranes (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Her-

cules, CA, USA). Then, the membranes were incubated overnight with

primary antibody of NUDFS1, ATP5C1, SDHC, ATP5J, or actin (as the

internal standard; Sigma–Aldrich). Bound antibody was detected by

chemiluminescence (ECL plus kit; Amersham BioSciences, Bucking-

hamshire, UK). The images were acquired by multispectral imaging sys-

tem (UVP BioSpectrum 815 Imaging System) and analyzed by Vision

Works LS software (UVP, California, USA).

3.1
Testicular TD-induced testicular injury and
disruption of testicular mitochondrial morphology

Histological findings showed moderate testicular injury in the ipsilat-

eral testis of TD group (mean injury score 10) and normal to mild

injury in the ipsilateral testis of Sham group (mean injury score 1.8)

(Figure S2). The injury score was significantly higher in TD group than
Sham group in ipsilateral testicular tissues (p < 0.001; Figure S2). In

contralateral testicular tissues, normal to mild testicular injury was

noted in Sham (mean injury score 1) and TD (mean injury score 1.8)

groups (Figure S2).

In the ipsilateral testicular tissues, normal mitochondrial morphol-

ogy was observed in the Sham group (Figure 1A), and both deforma-

tion of the mitochondrial cristae and inner mitochondrial membrane

disruption were noted in the TD group (Figure 1A). The number of dis-

rupted mitochondria was significantly higher in the TD group than in
the Sham group (p < 0.001; Figure 1B). In the contralateral testes, the

numbers of intact and disrupted mitochondria in testicular tissue were
not significantly difference between Sham and TD group (p = 0.1014;

Figure 1A). These results indicated that detorsion of the ischemic testis

caused mitochondrial damage in the twisted ipsilateral testes but not in

the untwisted contralateral testes.

An intact mitochondrial morphology was preserved in the mid-

piece region of the sperm cells in the ipsilateral testis of Sham group

(Figure 1C). In the midpiece region of the sperm cells in the ipsilat-

eral twisted testicular tissues, disruption of the inner mitochondrial

membrane was noted (Figure 1C). In the ipsilateral testes, the num-

ber of disrupted mitochondria was significantly higher in the TD group
than in the Sham group (p < 0.001; Figure 1D). In the contralateral

testes, the numbers of intact and disrupted mitochondria did not dif-
fer significantly between the groups (p = 0.132; Figure 1D). These

results demonstrated that testicular TD induced mitochondrial mor-

phology disruption in sperm cells in the twisted ipsilateral testes; how-

ever, mitochondrial morphology remained unchanged in the untwisted

contralateral testes.

Our results demonstrated that testicular TD reduced germ-cell res-

piratory function and affected those dysregulated proteins that are

related to mitochondrial OXPHOS in germ cells. According to previ-

ous studies, defective mitochondrial respiratory function in germ cells
results in spermatogenesis impairment and infertility.8,26 Proper func-

tional mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes in sperm mitochon-
dria play a crucial role in sperm motility.27 Moreover, OXPHOS is also

a major source of energy for maintaining normal function of the sperm
motility.28 Asthenozoospermia has been reported as a possible conse-

quence after testicular TD, and subfertility is a serious consequence
that must be prevented.24 However, the role of germ cells in mitochon-

drial respiratory function remains unclear. Our findings clarified the

status of mitochondrial respiratory function in germ cells after testicu-

lar TD, suggesting that germ-cell OXPHOS dysfunction may play a role

in the pathogenesis of semen quality impairment after testicular TD.

been successfully used in animal models to overcome the ischemia–
reperfusion injury induced by testicular TD.2,29–31 However, no
