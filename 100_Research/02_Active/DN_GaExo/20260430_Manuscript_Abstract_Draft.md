# 📝 Manuscript Abstract Draft: DN_GaExo Project

**Title**: Garlic-Derived Exosome-like Nanoparticles Mitigate Diabetic Nephropathy by Modulating the Gut-Kidney Axis and Restoring Butyrate-Producing Bacteria

---

### **Abstract**

**Background**: 
Diabetic nephropathy (DN) remains a leading cause of end-stage renal disease worldwide, characterized by chronic inflammation and progressive fibrosis. Emerging evidence highlights the "gut-kidney axis" as a critical regulator of metabolic health. Garlic-derived exosome-like nanoparticles (GaExo) have shown potent anti-inflammatory properties, yet their therapeutic efficacy and underlying mechanisms in DN models remain largely unexplored.

**Methods**: 
C57BL/6 mice were fed a high-fat diet (HFD) combined with streptozotocin (STZ) to induce a progressive DN model. Mice were treated with two doses of GaExo (GaE9 and GaE10) for 10 weeks. Physiological parameters, serum biochemistry (BUN, AC, TG), and renal histopathology (Tubulointerstitial Injury Score, TIS) were longitudinal monitored. 16S rRNA sequencing was performed to analyze gut microbiota alterations and their correlation with renal function.

**Results**: 
DN model mice exhibited severe hyperglycemia (>600 mg/dL), significant weight loss (diabetic wasting), and profound renal dysfunction. By week 10, the HS10W group showed a dramatic rise in BUN (27.93 ± 0.62 mg/dL) and severe tubulointerstitial damage (TIS: 3.75 ± 0.07). Notably, GaExo treatment, particularly at the high dose (GaE10), significantly rescued renal function (BUN: 20.68 ± 1.30 mg/dL, p=0.0005) and mitigated pathological lesions (TIS: 1.60 ± 0.07, p<0.0001) without altering the high blood glucose background. Furthermore, GaExo markedly reduced serum triglycerides (TG: 189.6 vs. 456.0 mg/dL, p=0.0151). Microbiome analysis revealed that GaExo intervention restored the abundance of butyrate-producing bacteria, specifically *Roseburia*, which showed a strong negative correlation with BUN levels (Rho = -0.556, p = 0.0011).

**Conclusions**: 
Our findings demonstrate that GaExo exerts a potent dose-dependent protective effect against DN. This protection is likely mediated through the modulation of the gut-kidney axis, specifically by enriching butyrate-producing bacteria and reducing systemic lipidotoxicity, rather than through direct glycemic control. GaExo represents a promising, non-invasive therapeutic strategy for managing chronic diabetic kidney injury.
